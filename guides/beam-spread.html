Beam spread: estimate the light footprint

Compare narrow and wide beams at a fixed distance.

By Saiful Bahri · Worked illustrative example

Start with geometry

For a circular beam perpendicular to a flat surface, diameter is approximately twice the distance multiplied by the tangent of half the beam angle. The nominal angle is not the edge of all visible light.

Worked comparison

At 2 m from the surface, a 24° beam has a nominal diameter of 0.85 m; a 60° beam is about 2.31 m. The wider beam covers more area but the actual illuminance depends on the luminous intensity distribution, not just diameter.

Plan a real comparison

Measure the distance from the light to the surface, not the room height alone. Draw the two footprints on paper. Check whether the artwork or task extends outside either footprint. For a tilted spotlight the footprint is not circular.

Decision

Use the narrower candidate for a small target if the beam distribution and glare are acceptable. For broad tasks, compare actual photometric data and overlapping beams. Do not choose fixture spacing from diameter alone.
