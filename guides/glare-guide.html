Reduce glare and harsh shadows in a room

Fix uncomfortable light before buying brighter bulbs.

By LumaRoom EditorialPublished and reviewed: 5 Nov 2026Approx. 495 words

Recognize direct and reflected glare

Direct glare occurs when a bright source sits in your view, such as an exposed bulb above a dining table. Reflected glare appears on glossy desktops, televisions, polished counters, and screens. Both can make a space tiring even when its overall brightness is reasonable. Note the position from which the problem occurs.

Look from the user’s eye level

Sit where you read, type, eat, or watch television. Scan the field of view. Can you see a bare light source? Does a bright patch reflect from a desk or monitor? Ask someone to move a lamp while you watch the reflection. Moving a fixture slightly can improve comfort more than changing to a lower-watt bulb.

Diffuse and shield the source

A shade, diffuser, recessed placement, or wall bounce can soften bright points. Choose an enclosure and lamp combination designed to work together; do not add improvised materials near hot fixtures. Broad surfaces often provide calmer ambient illumination than visible high-output LEDs. Remember that diffusion can reduce light reaching the task, so reassess useful brightness afterward.

Reduce shadows at desks and counters

The shadow often comes from your body or hand blocking an overhead beam. Move the task source to the opposite side of the writing hand, or add under-cabinet light for kitchen preparation. Avoid putting a lamp where it shines into someone facing the work area. Multiple moderate sources can remove an awkward shadow.

Screens need a different check

Glare on a display is often about source placement relative to the reflective screen. Move lamps away from the reflection angle and consider indirect light behind or beside the screen. Keep ambient surroundings comfortable so the screen is not a glaring rectangle in an otherwise dark room.

Evaluate after changing one thing

Make one adjustment at a time: position, angle, shielding, output, or lamp type. If the problem persists despite simple changes, it may need a fixture designed with better optical control. Avoid replacing fixed wiring or modifying electrical fittings without a qualified professional.

Take this into the room

Before buying anything, test one light position, direction, or control change after sunset. For electrical installation, code compliance, or moisture-sensitive locations, follow manufacturer instructions and consult a qualified electrician.

Quick action checklist

  • Write down the task or visual problem you want to solve.
  • Observe the room from the actual working or seating position after dark.
  • Check brightness, glare, direction, and color appearance separately.
  • Confirm compatibility and suitability before buying or installing any product.
  • Reassess the result over several evenings.
