Room-by-room lighting audit you can do tonight

A practical checklist to turn lighting observations into a shopping plan.

By LumaRoom EditorialPublished and reviewed: 5 Nov 2026Approx. 455 words

Choose one room and a task

A useful audit starts with behavior rather than a fixture catalog. Pick a room and name its three most frequent activities. For example, a kitchen may be used for chopping, cleaning, and dining; a bedroom for reading, dressing, and moving safely at night. The activities determine which areas need attention.

Observe after sunset

Walk through the room when daylight is no longer masking shadows. Look from each seat and working position. Identify bright sources in direct view, reflected glare on screens, and spots where your body blocks light. Write down a problem before thinking about a purchase.

Review existing controls

Check whether the room can support multiple scenes through separate lamps or switches. A brighter bulb is rarely the only option. Moving a lamp, adding a task source, or changing a shade can improve comfort while preserving flexible use.

Prioritize three fixes

Rank changes by their impact on everyday tasks, not visual drama. First fix areas that are difficult to use or navigate safely. Next address glare. Finally consider accent light for ambiance. If electrical work is involved, have it designed or reviewed by a qualified electrician.

Use a purchase checklist

For any new bulb or fixture, confirm physical compatibility, light output, color appearance, color rendering, dimming support, environmental suitability, and warranty. Compare these factors in that order. Avoid buying multiple decorative products before you know whether they solve the observed problem.

Reassess after a week

Try the new arrangement during different activities for several evenings. If the result is still uncomfortable, describe the precise symptom rather than adding more general brightness. The planning tool on this site can help you organize an initial scheme, but it does not calculate code-required illuminance.

Take this into the room

Before buying anything, test one light position, direction, or control change after sunset. For electrical installation, code compliance, or moisture-sensitive locations, follow manufacturer instructions and consult a qualified electrician.

Quick action checklist

  • Write down the task or visual problem you want to solve.
  • Observe the room from the actual working or seating position after dark.
  • Check brightness, glare, direction, and color appearance separately.
  • Confirm compatibility and suitability before buying or installing any product.
  • Reassess the result over several evenings.
